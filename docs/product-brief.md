# Product Brief — OtherSide AI

## One-line idea

OtherSide AI is a website that turns any one-sided story into a neutral “other side” report.

## Problem

People often encounter a conflict through one tweet, one article, one speech, one historical narrative, or one viral claim. The first version they read becomes the default truth in their mind. Existing AI tools often summarize, explain, or judge. They do not consistently force the missing perspective into view.

## Solution

A calm AI research interface where the user pastes a story, claim, URL, tweet text, article excerpt, or historical account. The AI identifies:

- the claim being made,
- who is making it,
- who is being criticized or opposed,
- what the other party would likely argue,
- which parts are agreed facts,
- which parts are disputed,
- what sources are needed or available.

The app avoids giving its own verdict.

## Product promise

No judgment. No winner. No moral lecture. Only the missing side.

## Target users

- Researchers
- Students
- Journalists
- Business leaders
- Lawyers and legal researchers
- History learners
- People following public disputes
- Anyone who wants to avoid one-sided understanding

## Core use cases

### Business conflict
User pastes an Elon Musk statement about OpenAI. The app explains OpenAI’s strongest counter-position using public statements, filings, and context.

### Historical narrative
User pastes one empire’s version of a historical event. The app explains the local, opposing, or affected group’s narrative.

### Political speech
User pastes a politician’s claim. The app explains the counter-position from the criticized party or affected stakeholders, without endorsing either side.

### Company dispute
User pastes a founder/investor/customer complaint. The app explains how the company may respond and what facts are disputed.

## Differentiation

Other AI tools answer broad questions. OtherSide AI enforces a narrow behavior: it does not act as a judge. It acts as a counter-story research assistant.

## Non-goals for MVP

- Do not build social debate features.
- Do not rank who is right.
- Do not create a public comment war platform.
- Do not generate inflammatory political persuasion.
- Do not claim perfect neutrality.
- Do not hide uncertainty.

## Success criteria

The MVP is successful if a user can paste a one-sided claim and receive a useful, structured, source-aware counter-position in under one minute.
