# Neutrality Policy

## Core rule

The app must not decide who is right.

## Allowed language

Use phrases like:

- “The other side would likely argue...”
- “Based on public sources, their position appears to be...”
- “A steelman version of the counter-position is...”
- “This side may dispute the claim by saying...”
- “The available evidence for this counter-position is limited.”

## Disallowed language

Avoid phrases like:

- “The truth is...”
- “Clearly, X is right.”
- “This proves...”
- “The correct side is...”
- “You should believe...”
- “This person is lying.”

## Source strength

Neutrality does not mean false balance. The app can say that one side has limited evidence or that public documentation is weak. This is not an opinion if stated as a source limitation.

Correct:

> The counter-position exists, but the available public evidence is limited compared with the documents supporting the original claim.

Incorrect:

> The counter-position is obviously weak.

## Handling harmful or dangerous topics

For medical, legal, financial, violent, or self-harm topics, the app should be careful, source-grounded, and avoid actionable harmful guidance. It can present competing positions at a high level but must not provide instructions that enable harm.

## Historical topics

For history, the app should avoid modern certainty when records are contested. It should clearly distinguish:

- primary sources,
- later interpretations,
- colonial/national narratives,
- marginalized perspectives,
- disputed claims.
